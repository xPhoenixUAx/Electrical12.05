EV and energy

EV charging and energy upgrades that start with capacity, not just hardware.

A charger install is more than mounting a box. The provider needs service size, panel capacity, charger amperage, wire path, garage or exterior location, permitting expectations, and whether future backup power or load management matters.

Services included

Real equipment, real load planning.

This group covers EV chargers, load calculations, panel readiness, conduit routing, disconnects, smart load management, and backup-power preparation. The goal is a practical installation plan rather than a generic charger quote.

  • Level 2 EV charger installation
    Providers may install hardwired chargers or charger receptacles after reviewing manufacturer instructions, circuit rating, breaker type, conductor size, mounting height, working clearance, and weather rating where applicable.
  • Load calculation and panel capacity
    Before installation, providers review service size, existing HVAC, range, dryer, water heater, pool, workshop, and other major loads. A load calculation may determine whether a panel upgrade or load management device is needed.
  • Conduit and wire path planning
    Routing can include garage walls, basements, crawlspaces, attics, exterior conduit, trench coordination, or detached structures. Providers consider support, bends, protection from damage, moisture exposure, and finished-wall repair limits.
  • Subpanels and breaker space
    If the main panel is full or poorly located, the provider may discuss a subpanel, tandem limitations, service upgrade, breaker compatibility, or panel replacement as a separate scope.
  • Backup power readiness
    Requests can include transfer switches, generator inlets, essential-load panels, interlock discussions, or battery-ready planning. Providers confirm what equipment is allowed locally and what must be inspected.
  • Load management and smart controls
    Some homes need a load-shedding device or smart charger settings to prevent overloading the service. The provider confirms compatible equipment and practical operating limits.
Field context

Placement, amperage, and route determine the real scope.

A charger near the panel may be simple. A detached garage, finished walls, exterior run, full panel, or high-amperage charger changes the work. Photos of the panel, charger location, parking position, and route help reduce back-and-forth.

The site coordinates intake only. The assigned independent provider confirms final diagnosis, materials, permits, pricing, warranty, and schedule.
Capacity and equipment planning

The charger location is only one part of the electrical scope.

EV work should capture charger model, desired amperage, panel rating, available breaker spaces, major existing loads, parking position, route length, wall material, indoor or outdoor exposure, and whether the homeowner expects future battery, generator, or second-charger work. Those details help the provider decide between a direct circuit, load management, subpanel, or panel upgrade discussion.

  • Load review: HVAC, range, dryer, water heater, pool equipment, and other large loads are considered before charger amperage is confirmed.
  • Route planning: conduit, finished walls, exterior exposure, detached garages, trenching, and working clearances affect labor and materials.
  • Commissioning: settings, breaker rating, manufacturer instructions, inspection requirements, and customer handoff are confirmed.
How the work is approached

Methods, tools, and decisions providers commonly use.

The exact method depends on the building, equipment, local code, and provider judgment, but these are the practical checkpoints that make the request clearer.

Site details collected

The request captures charger model, desired amperage, panel photos, service rating, parking location, route length, wall material, Wi-Fi needs, and indoor or outdoor exposure.

Capacity review

The provider compares charger demand with existing loads and panel condition, then discusses circuit size, breaker compatibility, load management, or upgrade needs.

Mounting and wiring

Installation may include secure charger mounting, conduit, conductors, fittings, breaker installation, disconnects where needed, and manufacturer-required commissioning steps.

Permit and inspection path

Many charger installations require a permit or inspection. The provider confirms local requirements, final scope, warranty, and any utility or rebate documentation.

Standards and verification

What should be confirmed before work is approved.

Electrical requirements vary by jurisdiction and property type. These pages describe common coordination needs; the independent provider confirms the enforceable requirements for the actual site.

  • NEC and local code requirements for EV supply equipment, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, GFCI requirements, and working clearance must be confirmed by the provider.
  • Listed charger equipment should be installed according to manufacturer instructions and location ratings.
  • Outdoor chargers, detached garages, trenching, or service upgrades may require additional permitting and utility coordination.
  • The homeowner should verify provider licensing, insurance, permit responsibility, and inspection completion before approving work.
