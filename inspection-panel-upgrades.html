Panel capacity, safety concerns, and code corrections explained before work begins.

Services included

For properties that need more than a quick repair.

This group covers inspection requests, panel replacements, subpanels, breaker issues, grounding and bonding concerns, surge protection, and code correction lists from inspectors, insurance companies, property managers, or remodel teams.

  • Panel replacement and service upgrades
    Requests may involve aging panels, limited breaker space, damaged buses, obsolete equipment, added loads, remodel needs, or insurance concerns. Providers confirm service size, utility coordination, permit requirements, grounding, and whether a full upgrade is needed.
  • Subpanels and circuit organization
    Subpanels may help garages, additions, workshops, or equipment-heavy areas. Providers evaluate feeder size, grounding and bonding separation, breaker compatibility, panel location, and working clearance.
  • Whole-home surge protection
    Surge protection requests involve equipment selection, breaker space, grounding condition, panel compatibility, and protection goals for appliances, electronics, HVAC equipment, and EV chargers.
  • Safety inspections
    Inspection-style requests can include open junctions, missing covers, double-tapped conductors, overheating marks, poor labeling, old devices, damaged cable, improper splices, or ungrounded circuits.
  • Code correction lists
    Providers can review correction notes from home inspectors, municipalities, insurers, landlords, or renovation teams and separate electrical defects from cosmetic or non-electrical items.
  • Breaker replacement and labeling
    Breaker work requires panel-compatible parts, load identification, correct ratings, labeling, and review of why the breaker failed or tripped before simply replacing it.
Field context

Panel work starts with existing conditions.

Useful intake photos include the full panel, open breaker view if safe and allowed, service label, meter area, grounding electrode area, subpanels, and any inspector notes. The provider then confirms whether the work is repair, upgrade, correction, or replacement.

The site coordinates intake only. The assigned independent provider confirms final diagnosis, materials, permits, pricing, warranty, and schedule.
Panel upgrade preparation

Panel work depends on compatibility, grounding, permits, and utility steps.

A provider may need panel brand and model, service rating, meter photos, grounding electrode details, subpanel locations, major loads, inspector notes, and future equipment plans. The final scope may be a repair, breaker correction, subpanel, surge protection, full panel replacement, service upgrade, or utility-coordinated project.

  • Compatibility: breakers and accessories must be listed or approved for the panel, and damaged equipment may require replacement.
  • Grounding and bonding: grounding electrodes, bonding jumpers, neutral-ground separation, and surge protection path are reviewed.
  • Permit path: service upgrades commonly require permit, inspection, meter coordination, and utility disconnect/reconnect scheduling.
How the work is approached

Methods, tools, and decisions providers commonly use.

The exact method depends on the building, equipment, local code, and provider judgment, but these are the practical checkpoints that make the request clearer.

Photo and document review

The request should include panel photos, labels, inspector reports, utility notes, appliance additions, remodel plans, and any deadlines tied to insurance, sale, or inspection.

Capacity and condition check

Providers may review service rating, breaker compatibility, bus condition, grounding, bonding, conductor size, available spaces, and major existing loads.

Permit and utility coordination

Panel replacement and service upgrades often require permits, inspection, meter coordination, utility scheduling, and clear responsibility for who handles each step.

Upgrade recommendation

The provider confirms whether the issue is a repair, panel cleanup, subpanel, service upgrade, surge protection, or broader correction plan.

Standards and verification

What should be confirmed before work is approved.

Electrical requirements vary by jurisdiction and property type. These pages describe common coordination needs; the independent provider confirms the enforceable requirements for the actual site.

  • Panel, service, grounding, bonding, breaker, and surge protection work must align with applicable NEC and local authority requirements.
  • Listed breakers and panel components should match the panel manufacturer and approved compatibility rules.
  • Service upgrades may require utility disconnect/reconnect, meter work, permits, and inspections.
  • Final scope, code interpretation, price, warranty, and permit responsibility must be confirmed directly with the independent provider.
